## Evidence Landscape

The work undertaken during the placement was informed by established evidence relating to health and wellbeing, with particular emphasis on prevention, 
healthy ageing and the use of health data. Throughout the placement, I drew primarily on two sources to inform my understanding of the work and the decisions I made.

### Ikarians' internal clinical reference material

Ikarians has an internal clinical knowledge base covering areas including physical activity, sleep, mental health and stress, nutrition, spirituality, social relationships and longevity.
The material draws on peer-reviewed research and clinical guidance, including sources from organisations and journals such as the AHA, ACSM, JAMA, NEJM and WHO.

I have not included this document in the repository because it is proprietary Ikarians material and was not produced by me as part of the placement.
Where it informed my work, particularly in relation to data quality and AI-readiness, I have expressed the relevant reasoning in my own words and supported it with publicly available peer-reviewed literature. 
This maintains a clear distinction between the company's internal knowledge base and my own work.

### Reading list

Alongside the internal clinical material, the founding team shared a curated reading list, available at [`../06-supporting-evidence/DOCS_OF_INTERESTS.docx`](../06-supporting-evidence/DOCS_OF_INTERESTS.docx). 
It covers the broader thinking behind Ikarians' product, including:

* value-based healthcare and the shift from treating disease towards prevention;
* holistic and longevity-focused approaches to health, including McKinsey's *Adding Years to Life and Life to Years* and the Blue Zones literature;
* drivers of healthy life expectancy and the social determinants of health, including UK Government and Healthy People 2030 material;
* clinical text datasets and medical AI/LLMs, which were particularly relevant to the AI-readiness work later in the placement; and
* Global Burden of Disease data and projections.

Two themes from this wider reading were particularly relevant throughout the placement. First, the value of collecting data that could ultimately support the evaluation of meaningful health outcomes, rather than relying solely on measures such as engagement. 
Second, the challenges involved in preparing health data for AI applications, particularly given the complexity, heterogeneity and variability of health information.

Together, these sources provided the evidence base for my work and helped me approach the placement from a health data science perspective. 
Rather than treating health and wellbeing as a set of isolated measures, the evidence encouraged me to consider how data quality, completeness and structure affect the ability to understand meaningful health outcomes. 
This became particularly relevant to my work on AI-readiness, where the usefulness of health data depends not only on the quantity of information collected but also on its consistency, interpretability and suitability for future analysis. 
In this way, the wider evidence on prevention, healthy ageing and health data provided context for the practical data challenges I encountered during the placement.
